## The arbitration this ticket implemented

**(A) A reported outcome replaces an empty one.** `Streams::record` no longer keeps the first outcome it is handed: an `Empty` conclusion may be replaced by a reported one, upserted in place under the terminal entry's own key rather than appending a second conclusion. Narrow in both directions — only `Empty` may be replaced, and only by something that is not `Empty` — so a child that reported is still closed to a provider that goes on narrating it. Not a reopening: the child stays terminal throughout and gains no ordinary work.

**The consolidation.** "No _new_ entry after a conclusion, revisions of existing keys excepted" now lives in `Streams::record`, where it survives a restart, instead of in the Codex adapter's in-memory guard, which did not. `Subagent::keys` and `Subagent::concluded` are gone; with nothing left for it to refuse, `Children::still_open` became `Children::about`, which cannot fail, and its three callers lost their `let … else` guards. The Standards finding ticket 04 recorded is closed with them.

**One exception was added deliberately, and it is not (B).** A **nested launcher** may enter a concluded stream, because it is the only place a descendant is shown and the adapter has already withheld that descendant's root row on the strength of the same parentage — refusing it would leave the descendant visible nowhere. It is inserted _in front of_ the conclusion, which is the one case where an entry's sequence moves, so that story 15's "the result last in the stream" stays true. `insert_before_the_conclusion` carries the argument.

**(B) A concluded child may reopen on proven provider evidence** is **not** implemented and remains deferred to its own ticket. Nothing here needed it.

## Why the toast guard is the honest maximum

Criterion 9's negative half is a **prohibition**, and a prohibition cannot be discharged by construction — there is no artifact to build. Its only two possible proofs are an audit that no producer exists, which is a fact about the tree at a point in time and rots, and an executable guard that fails the moment somebody adds one. `SubagentAutoOpen.test.tsx::raises no toast as a child finishes, fails or is stopped` is the second, placed where such a change would actually land: it drives a real compact child row through `completed`, `failed` and `stopped` with `toastManager.add` spied. "Tell me when a child finishes" is a reasonable-sounding future request, and this is what turns it red.

Demanding more — a test of a _suppression_ mechanism — would require building a per-child notification pathway in order to prove it stays silent, which is what Out of Scope forbids: "Toasts or desktop notifications for individual child completion."

The audit, as of this ticket, for whoever wants the starting point rather than the conclusion: no `Notification`, `requestPermission` or `sendNotification` anywhere in `apps/web/src` or `packages/client-runtime/src`; `laplus-shell/src/main.rs` registers only `tauri_plugin_updater` and `generate_handler![network_access_state, set_network_exposure]`, so the desktop shell exposes no notification command the page could call; and no toast call site sits in any subagent path. **Correction to an earlier claim in this ticket's history:** `toastManager` is _not_ called from only two places — there are on the order of 150 `toastManager.add` sites across the app. What is true, and what matters, is that none of them is keyed to subagent state.

## Known properties a later reader should not mistake for bugs

- **A restored child is not a running one.** A stream left non-terminal on disk records what the last run was told; the process behind it is gone, so it does not count toward the conversation's Working state and a Stop does not rewrite it. Its own tab and compact row still report exactly what the provider last said, because that is what was recorded — this is a read-time claim about liveness, not an edit of durable history. A child of a provider that genuinely outlived laplus (an external OpenCode server is the real case) counts again the moment it says anything, which is the only evidence either way.
- **A root that ended `interrupted`, `stopped` or `error` is left alone**, even with a live descendant, so the conversation is not reported Working. Deliberate: those statuses say something the developer needs and a delegation tree is not a reason to withhold it. It is also what stops a Stop being undone by whatever the provider narrates next.
- **`running` means "there is work here", not "there is a turn here".** `CONTEXT.md`'s **Session status** and **Settling** entries carry the amendment; the client's reducer folds a `running` session naming no turn against a turn it has already settled and leaves that turn alone.
- **A Codex conversation cannot produce the "root settled, descendant still active" shape**, because its children conclude inside the turn that waits on them. The Working-state criterion is therefore proven on Claude and OpenCode; Codex's half of it is the leaving — `stopping_a_codex_parent_stops_the_generation_below_it_too` asserts the conversation is not working once its tree is.
